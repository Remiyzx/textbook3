🎵 Third-tone sandhi (Pg 36)

A 3rd tone, when followed by a 1st, 2nd, 4th, or most neutral tones, usually becomes a half third tone (only falls but does not rise).

⚠️ Tone sandhi of "bù" (Pg 36)

"bù" is a 4th tone. But it becomes a 2nd tone (bú) when followed by another 4th tone!

Normal (bù): bù hē, bù máng, bù hǎo
Change to 2nd (bú): bú shì, bú yào

Grammar & Culture Focus

1. Identifying People & Asking Nationality

👤 Asking Identity (shéi)

Nà shì shéi?

(Who is that?)

Nà shì wǒmen lǎoshī.

(That is our teacher.)

🌍 Asking Nationality (nǎ)

Tā shì nǎ guó rén?

(Which country is she from?)

Tā shì Zhōngguó rén.

(She is a Chinese person.)

🔍 New 5W1H Question Words!

shéi (WHO): Used to ask about a person's identity.

nǎ (WHICH): Used to ask for a selection from a group.
Important Rule: nǎ MUST always be followed by a Measure Word or a Noun (like nǎ guó - which country).

🌎 Culture Note: Translating Country Names

1. Phonetic matching + Flattering Meanings

Many names are transliterated phonetically, but the characters chosen carry flattering or positive literal meanings.

  • 🇺🇸 Měiguó ("Mei" sounds like A-me-ri-ca. Literally: "Beautiful Country")
  • 🇬🇧 Yīngguó ("Ying" sounds like Eng-land. Literally: "Heroic Country")
  • 🇫🇷 Fǎguó ("Fa" sounds like Fran-ce. Literally: "Law Country")
  • 🇩🇪 Déguó ("De" sounds like Deut-schland. Literally: "Virtue Country")

2. Direct Transliteration

Based directly on the original pronunciation of the country's name.

  • 🇲🇾 Mǎláixīyà (Malaysia)
  • 🇨🇦 Jiānádà (Canada)
  • 🇯🇵 Rìběn (Japan)
  • 🇸🇬 Xīnjiāpō (Singapore)

To indicate nationality, add rén (person)!

Zhōngguó + rén = Chinese person / Chinese people

Mǎláixīyà + rén = Malaysian

⚠️ The ambiguity of "Chinese"

When translating into Chinese, be precise!

  • • Zhōngguó rén = Nationality (Citizen of China)
  • • Huárén = Ethnicity (e.g., Mǎláixīyà Huárén / Malaysian Chinese)
  • • Zhōngwén / Hànyǔ = The Chinese Language

💡 Understanding shì (to be) ➡️ "ialah"

A common mistake is translating the English "am/is/are" directly into "shì".
Instead, think of "shì" as "ialah" in Malay! If a sentence in Malay doesn't use "ialah" (like describing an adjective), DO NOT use "shì".

She is a teacher ➔ Dia ialah guru

Tā shì lǎoshī ✅

I am busy ➔ Saya sibuk (No ialah)

Wǒ shì máng

Wǒ máng ✅

Culture & Grammar Focus

🙏 The Polite "You" (nín)

Nín is the respectful form of nǐ. Use it when addressing:

  • • Older people or elders.
  • • Teachers or high-ranking officials.
  • • Anyone you want to show politeness to during a first meeting.

🧑‍🏫 Addressing Professionals

In Chinese, position or occupation is used as a title, and the surname always precedes the title.

English Manager Yang
VS
Chinese Yáng jīnglǐ

❌ Never address a teacher by their personal name!

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family Members: Paternal vs. Maternal

Notice how the maternal (mother's) side frequently uses the word wài (which literally means "outside").

Father's Side

  • yéye (Father's father)
  • nǎinai (Father's mother)
  • sūnzi (Son's son)
  • sūnnǚ (Son's daughter)

Mother's Side

  • wàigōng (Mother's father)
  • wàipó (Mother's mother)
  • wàisūn (Daughter's son)
  • wàisūnnǚ (Daughter's daughter)
  • wàishēng (Sister's son)
  • wàishēngnǚ (Sister's daughter)

Core Grammar Summary

🔍 5W1H Question Words (shéi & nǎ)

  • • shéi (Who): Inquires about someone's identity.
    (Nà shì shéi? ➔ Nà shì wǒmen lǎoshī.)
  • • nǎ (Which): Inquires about choice/selection. Must precede a measure word or noun!
    (Tā shì nǎ guó rén?)

🙏 Respectful Form (nín)

The polite, respectful form of "nǐ".

Always use when addressing elders, teachers, or showing high courtesy upon a first meeting.

💡 "shì" vs "ialah"

Only use "shì" when the structure translates to "ialah" in Malay.

Never use "shì" with plain adjectives!
Wǒ máng ✅ (Saya sibuk) vs Wǒ shì máng ❌.

⚠️ Tone Sandhi of "bù"

bú shì & bú yào

"bù" changes from 4th tone to 2nd tone (bú) before another 4th tone syllable.
